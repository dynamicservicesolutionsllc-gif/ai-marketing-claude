Before you buy, check these

  1. Fridge and freezer space: Measure shelf height and depth; square containers and stackable shapes waste less room than round ones.
  2. How you reheat: Check the listing for microwave, oven and freezer notes on both the container and the lid.
  3. How far you carry lunch: Glass is heavier than plastic, so a long commute favors a lightweight set.
  4. Meals per week: Count the containers you need; a set of five covers workday lunches, larger sets suit batch cooking.

Buy one matching set rather than mixing brands, so lids and containers always pair up.

Who should choose, or skip, each type

Glass containers

Glass sets with snap-lock lids handle tomato sauce, curry and reheated meals without staining, and suit cooking and storing in one dish.

Choose this type if:

  • You reheat meals at home
  • Stained plastic bothers you

Skip it if:

  • You carry lunch a long way
  • You drop things often

Plastic containers

Lightweight sets with latching lids travel well and cost little space in a lunch bag.

Choose this type if:

  • You commute with your meals
  • You want something shatter-resistant

Skip it if:

  • You often store tomato-based sauces

Large mixed sets

Twenty-piece sets give you enough matching containers to portion a week of meals at once.

Choose this type if:

  • You prep for several people
  • You freeze batches

Skip it if:

  • You have a tiny cabinet

Buying guide: what to look for

Match lids to containers

Lost lids are the most common reason meal prep falls apart. Buy one matching set and keep lids upright in an organizer.

Cool food before you seal it

Let hot food cool for a short while before closing the lid, then refrigerate promptly.

Pick shapes that stack

Square and rectangular containers use fridge shelves better than round ones.

Label and rotate

Write the date on a strip of tape so older meals get eaten first.

Frequently asked questions

Are glass or plastic meal prep containers better?

Glass resists stains and odors and works for reheating; plastic is lighter to carry. Many people keep glass for home and plastic for the bag.

What are the best meal prep containers for beginners?

A set of five matching glass or plastic containers with snap or latching lids covers workday lunches. Add more sizes later.

Can you freeze meal prep containers?

Check each listing: some sets are listed as freezer safe. Leave a little headspace for liquids, and see our freezer tray checklist for soup and sauce portions.

How many meal prep containers do I need?

Five covers a workweek of lunches; double that if you also prep dinners or freeze batches.
