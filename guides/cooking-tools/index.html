Before you buy, check these

  1. What you cook most: Baking points to a scale; roasts and poultry point to a thermometer.
  2. Units you use: Recipes in grams need a scale that switches units.
  3. Storage: A slim scale can live in a drawer; mitts need a hook or drawer.
  4. Heat exposure: Grill and oven work call for mitts rated for high heat.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a kitchen scale?

For baking and portioning, a scale is more consistent than measuring cups.

What temperature should chicken reach?

USDA lists 165°F as the safe minimum internal temperature for poultry.

What internal temperature should pork and beef reach?

USDA lists 145°F for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, with a 3-minute rest, and 160°F for ground meats.

Do I need to calibrate an instant-read thermometer?

It's worth checking occasionally in ice water, which should read 32°F. Follow the manufacturer's instructions if it can be adjusted.

Are silicone oven mitts better than cloth?

They resist steam and liquid much better. Cloth is more flexible, so some cooks keep both.
