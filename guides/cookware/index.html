Buying guide: what to look for

Match the pan to your burner

Check the base diameter against your burner, and confirm the cookware works with your cooktop type, including induction if you have it.

Nonstick, stainless or cast iron

Nonstick releases eggs and delicate foods easily but needs gentle tools. Stainless handles high heat and browning. Cast iron holds heat for searing and goes from stove to oven, but it is heavy and needs seasoning care.

Check the oven limit

Pans and lids list a maximum oven temperature. Stay under it, and check handles and knobs as well as the pan body.

Care keeps cookware working

Use soft sponges on nonstick, dry cast iron right after washing, and let enameled pots cool before rinsing.

Frequently asked questions

What cookware do I need in a small kitchen?

A nonstick frying pan for eggs, a larger skillet or stainless pan for searing and stir-fries, and one covered pot such as a Dutch oven handle most home cooking.

Is cast iron hard to take care of?

A pre-seasoned skillet needs only a quick rinse, a thorough dry and an occasional light coat of oil. Avoid long soaks.

Can a Dutch oven go in the oven?

Enameled cast iron pots are made for it, but check the maker's temperature limit for the pot and lid before baking bread or finishing a braise.

Does stainless steel work on induction?

Only if the maker lists it as induction compatible. The Tramontina pan here is listed as induction-ready.
