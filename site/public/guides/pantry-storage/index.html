Before you buy, measure these

  1. Shelf depth: Front edge to the back wall: deep shelves need bins or turntables to stay usable.
  2. Shelf height clearance: The gap between shelves, so stacked containers and can racks fit.
  3. Shelf width: How many bins or containers fit side by side.
  4. Door and trim: Door swing and any lip at the front of the shelf.
  5. What you buy in bulk: The size of the flour, sugar and cereal packages you need to decant.

Containers that hold a full bag of flour in one go save you from keeping a half-empty bag next to the canister.

Frequently asked questions

Are airtight containers worth it for a pantry?

For dry goods that go stale or attract pests, yes. Clear airtight containers also show when you're running low.

How do I organize a deep pantry shelf?

Use pull-out bins or a lazy Susan so items at the back are reachable without unloading the front.

Do I need to decant everything into containers?

No. Start with what goes stale or attracts pests: flour, sugar, rice, cereal, crackers. Leave sealed cans and jars as they are.

Are clear pantry containers worth it?

Being able to see what is running low is the main benefit, and it stops duplicate purchases.

How do I label pantry containers?

Use the included labels or a removable marker, and note the expiry date on the back for things you rotate slowly.
