Before you buy, check these

  1. What you cook most: Eggs and stir-fries point to a nonstick skillet; soups and chili to a Dutch oven; roasted vegetables and sheet-pan dinners to baking sheets.
  2. Your stovetop: Cast iron and stainless steel work on most cooktops; check the listing before using any pan on an induction burner.
  3. Your oven rack: Measure the rack so the largest baking sheet fits with room for air to move around it.
  4. Storage space: Nesting bowls, a lid that doubles as a skillet and a slim set of utensils keep a small kitchen from filling up.

Buy the first six tools before anything else. The rest can wait until a recipe calls for them.

Who should choose, or skip, each type

Cutting tools

A chef's knife, cutting boards, a peeler and kitchen shears handle almost all prep work.

Choose this type if:

  • You cook from fresh vegetables and meat
  • Your current knife is dull or flimsy

Skip it if:

  • You mostly heat pre-cut or frozen food

Pots and pans

A nonstick skillet, a small saucepan, a cast iron skillet and a Dutch oven cover nearly every recipe.

Choose this type if:

  • You're starting with no cookware
  • Your old pans are warped or sticky

Skip it if:

  • You already own a full matching set

Utensils and tongs

Wooden spoons, spatulas and silicone-tipped tongs move food around the pan without scratching it.

Choose this type if:

  • You just bought nonstick pans
  • Your utensils are mismatched or metal

Skip it if:

  • You only cook in stainless or cast iron and like metal tools

Measuring and mixing

Mixing bowls, measuring cups and a meat thermometer take the guesswork out of recipes.

Choose this type if:

  • You follow recipes closely
  • You're not sure when meat is done

Skip it if:

  • You already have bowls and cups that work

Buying guide: what to look for

Buy the core six first

A chef's knife, a cutting board, a nonstick skillet, a saucepan, cooking utensils and measuring cups cover most beginner recipes. Add the rest as you cook more.

Spend on what you use daily

The knife and the skillet get used at almost every meal, so they are worth choosing carefully; a peeler or can opener only has to work.

Mix pan materials

Nonstick for eggs and delicate food, cast iron for searing and oven use, stainless for sauces. One of each beats a big set of the same pan.

Pick tools that store small

Nesting bowls, a set of three boards and a Dutch oven with a skillet lid save space in a small or first-apartment kitchen.

Frequently asked questions

What are the basic kitchen essentials?

A chef's knife, a cutting board, a nonstick skillet, a saucepan with a lid, cooking utensils, measuring cups and mixing bowls. Tongs, a meat thermometer, baking sheets and a can opener are the next additions.

What kitchen tools does a beginner cook need?

Start with the tools that cover the most recipes: a knife and board for prep, a skillet and saucepan for cooking, utensils and tongs for stirring and flipping, and cups and bowls for measuring and mixing.

Should I buy a cookware set or individual pans?

For a beginner, a few individual pieces often work better than a large set: a nonstick skillet, a small saucepan, a cast iron skillet and a Dutch oven cover nearly everything, and each is chosen for its own job.

What should I buy first for a first apartment kitchen?

A chef's knife, a cutting board, a nonstick skillet and a saucepan let you cook most simple meals from the first night. Add utensils, measuring cups and bowls in the same order.
