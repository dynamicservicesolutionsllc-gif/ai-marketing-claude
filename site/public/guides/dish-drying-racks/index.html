Before you buy, measure these

  1. Counter space beside the sink: Width and depth of the spot where the rack will sit.
  2. Sink opening: For an over-the-sink rack, the shorter side of the sink opening.
  3. Faucet and backsplash: How far the faucet and wall are from where the rack would go.
  4. Your usual load: How many plates, glasses and pots you wash at once.

A rack that's sized for a full family's load wastes space if you cook for one or two. Size it to the dishes you actually wash in one go.

Who should choose, or skip, each type

Compact countertop rack with drainboard

The classic option, sized down. Our pick has a 12 by 15.5 inch footprint and a drainboard that catches drips.

Choose this type if:

  • You have a little counter beside the sink
  • You wash plates, bowls and glasses together
  • You want somewhere to leave dishes overnight

Skip it if:

  • Your counter is already full
  • You only wash a few items at a time

Roll-up over-the-sink rack

Spans the sink so dishes and produce drip straight into the basin. Our pick fits sinks whose shorter side is 17 inches or less and supports up to 100 lbs.

Choose this type if:

  • You have no counter space next to the sink
  • You also want a spot to rinse produce or rest hot pots
  • You like to put everything away after drying

Skip it if:

  • You need the sink free while dishes dry
  • Your sink is wider than the rack's length

Silicone drying mat

A ribbed mat lifts dishes off pooled water and its raised edges keep drips off the counter. Our pick is 18 by 16 inches and also fits under a dish rack.

Choose this type if:

  • You wash a few dishes at a time
  • Your current rack leaves puddles on the counter

Skip it if:

  • You air-dry a full family load every night

Over-the-sink frame rack

Freestanding frames with several tiers over the sink. We don't list one yet; check the frame's height against your faucet and any cabinets above.

Choose this type if:

  • You want several shelves of drying space
  • You have clear wall space above the sink

Skip it if:

  • You have upper cabinets or a window right over the sink

Picks for specific situations

Dish rack for a tiny kitchen with no counter space

Go over the sink, not beside it. A roll-up rack spans the basin and rolls up into a drawer when you're done.

Dish drying rack for a single-basin sink

A roll-up rack covers part of the basin while dishes dry, so you can still use the uncovered part, or roll it back when you need the full sink.

Dish rack for one or two people

A compact countertop rack is usually plenty. Look for a removable utensil holder and a drainboard so water doesn't pool on the counter.

Buying guide: what to look for

Countertop or over the sink

Countertop racks hold more upright plates; roll-up racks free the counter but need a sink that matches their width.

Check the drainage

A drainboard or a rack over the basin keeps puddles off the counter.

Measure the sink

For over-the-sink racks, measure the shorter side of the sink opening.

Frequently asked questions

What dish rack takes up the least counter space?

An over-the-sink roll-up rack uses no counter space at all, as long as it fits your sink.

Is a roll-up rack strong enough for pots?

Check the stated weight capacity; steel-core racks generally handle cookware.

Do roll-up dish racks sag with heavy pots?

They're designed to span the sink and carry weight, but each has a stated limit. Check the current listing, and avoid resting very heavy cast iron on the middle of the span.

How do I keep a dish rack from getting moldy?

Empty the drainboard daily, let the rack dry fully, and wash it with hot soapy water every week or two.

Where should a dish rack go in a small kitchen?

As close to the sink as possible so water drains back toward it, and away from outlets and the stovetop.
