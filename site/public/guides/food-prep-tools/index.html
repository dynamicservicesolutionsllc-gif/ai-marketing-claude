Who should choose, or skip, each type

Multi-blade chopper

Dices an onion or pepper with one press. Our pick has eight swappable blades for slicing, julienne and grating.

Choose this type if:

  • You cook soups, chili or salsa often
  • You want uniform pieces

Skip it if:

  • You rarely chop vegetables
  • You have little cabinet space

Compact dicer

A simpler press chopper with a non-skid base and parts that go on the dishwasher's top rack.

Choose this type if:

  • You mainly dice and want fewer pieces
  • You want a non-skid base

Skip it if:

  • You need slicing and grating too

Single-job helper

Tools like a chicken shredder, apple peeler or salad spinner take one slow job off your hands.

Choose this type if:

  • You shred chicken, peel apples or wash greens every week

Skip it if:

  • You do that task only occasionally

Buying guide: what to look for

Pick by your bottleneck

If dicing onions slows you down, start with a chopper; if shredding chicken does, a shredder.

Check the cleaning

Removable, dishwasher-safe parts make a gadget far more likely to get used.

Storage size

Choppers with catch containers take more cabinet room than hand tools.

Frequently asked questions

Are vegetable choppers worth it?

For frequent dicing of onions, peppers and similar vegetables, they save time and keep pieces uniform.

What is the easiest way to shred chicken?

A twist-lid chicken shredder handles cooked chicken faster than two forks.

Are vegetable choppers safe to use?

Use the pusher or lid, not your fingers, on the blade grid, and clean blades with a brush rather than by hand.

Can a vegetable chopper replace a food processor?

For dicing small batches, often yes. For dough, purees or large batches, a food processor is still faster.

How do I clean a press chopper?

Rinse right after use, push trapped pieces out with the included cleaning tool, then wash the parts on the top rack if the listing allows it.
