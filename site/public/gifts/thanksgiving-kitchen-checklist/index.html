How to choose the right gift

Plan your oven and stovetop time

Write down what needs the oven and when. Anything that can move to a slow cooker, like mashed potatoes, stuffing or cider, frees up space for the turkey.

Check the turkey with a thermometer

Pop-up timers and cooking charts are rough guides. A probe thermometer in the thickest part of the thigh tells you when it's actually done.

Make the gravy while the turkey rests

Pour the pan drippings through a fat separator, let the fat rise for a minute, then pour the juices from the bottom for gravy.

Order early

Delivery gets slower and popular items sell out as the holiday gets closer. Ordering a few weeks ahead gives you time for a practice run.

Frequently asked questions

What kitchen tools do I need to host Thanksgiving?

A meat thermometer, a roasting pan, a fat separator for gravy, oven mitts that handle a heavy pan, and containers for leftovers cover most of the day. A slow cooker helps when the oven is full.

How do I keep side dishes warm on Thanksgiving?

A slow cooker on its warm setting holds mashed potatoes, stuffing or gravy at serving temperature without taking up a burner or the oven.

How do I know when the turkey is done?

Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part of the thigh, without touching bone, and follow USDA guidance for poultry temperatures.
